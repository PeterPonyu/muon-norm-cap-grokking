\begin{abstract}
The Muon optimizer orthogonalizes each parameter update and, on small algorithmic
tasks, sharply shortens the grokking delay---the long lag before a memorizing
network suddenly generalizes. Muon-trained networks also grow their hidden-weight
norms rather than contracting them, but the existing evidence linking this growth
to the mechanism is correlational. We test the causal reading directly: on a shared
420k-parameter two-layer transformer we impose a downward-only Frobenius ceiling,
capping hidden-weight-norm growth at $k\cdot\|W\|_{\mathrm{init}}$ without
inflating weights or cancelling weight decay. Suppressing the growth never delays
or blocks grokking at any ceiling down to $k=1$, and on the non-abelian $S_5$ task
it accelerates grokking by roughly an order of magnitude (median grok step
$3525\to325$; seed-level bootstrap 95\% percentile CI $6.6$--$16.7\times$), with a
dose-response monotone in the ceiling. This preserve-and-accelerate pattern
replicates across tasks, widths, depths, and modular-addition scales, and a
learning-rate crossing control shows the acceleration is a property of the
(optimizer, learning-rate-regime) pair rather than an artifact of rate assignment.
Weight-norm growth is therefore dispensable for Muon's fast grokking---its removal
accelerates it---and, on the task where the optimizer choice is decisive, the
optimizer-dependence reduces to the spectrum-flattening geometry of the update:
on $S_5$ an exact-SVD flattening reproduces Muon while energy-matched random
semi-orthogonal and spectrum-inverted updates never grok. A growth-floor argument
explains why orthogonalized updates force monotone norm growth under Muon and not
AdamW. Bounding dissociations and a real-text arm---where the same cap costs
validation loss dose-dependently---delimit the account's scope: preserve-and-accelerate
is a grokking-regime result, not a general norm-control recipe.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{4pt}\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} grokking; Muon; orthogonalized updates; weight decay; training dynamics; implicit bias; mode connectivity; grokking delay

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Motivation and gap.}
Grokking---the phenomenon where a network first memorizes then, much later, suddenly
generalizes \citep{power2022grokking}---has become a sharp lens for studying the
dynamics of generalization. Weight decay ($\lambda$) is the canonical clock: Omnigrok
\citep{liu2023omnigrok} shows that constraining weight norms to a sphere speeds
grokking, and a growing body of work ties the delay to norm growth under decoupled
weight decay \citep{loshchilov2019adamw,liu2023omnigrok,khanh2026normseparation,thilak2022slingshot}.
Mechanistic interpretability work
on modular arithmetic \citep{nanda2023progress} reveals discrete Fourier circuits as
the generalizing algorithm, with the ``excluded loss'' and ``restricted loss'' as
continuous progress measures that underlie the sharp accuracy transition.
Against this backdrop, \citet{tveit2025muongrokking} showed that Muon---an optimizer
that orthogonalizes each update via Newton--Schulz iteration \citep{jordan2024muon,bernstein2024modular}---sharply
accelerates grokking, and competing mechanistic
stories have been advanced: a flat/uniform-spectrum
dynamics account \citep{beneventano2026spectraldynamics}, and a skeptical view that
the precise spectral geometry is not what matters---alignment and step-size optimality
suffice \citep{shumaylov2026muonnotspecial}.
What is missing is twofold. First, an interventional test of whether Muon's
empirically observed norm growth is the mechanism behind faster grokking or merely
a byproduct of the orthogonalized update---the existing evidence is correlational.
Second, a cross-phenomenon characterization of how the growth route differs
from AdamW's contraction route across related phenomena ($S_5$ non-abelian grokking,
linear mode connectivity (LMC), massive activations, and continual-learning plasticity),
which would show whether the route is a coherent signature or a one-task artifact.
We supply both.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We give a cross-phenomenon empirical signature of a non-contraction
``growth'' route on a shared testbed, with AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}
(contraction baseline) and SGDM (plain momentum) controls, and---where the data
support it---a manipulated-cause arm.  Concretely:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Causal intervention on the norm.} A downward-only Frobenius ceiling
    $\|W\|\leq k\cdot\|W\|_{\mathrm{init}}$ preserves grokking 8/8 at every $k$
    including $k=1$ and accelerates it ${\sim}10\times$ on $S_5$ (grok step $3525\to325$;
    bootstrap 95\% percentile CI $6.6$--$16.7\times$, with bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) intervals for every
    ratio in Table~\ref{tab:ci}).
    On $S_5$ the acceleration reaches $15.8$--$38\times$ across a $4\times$ width
    range and at network depth 4 (largest at depth 4). The
    preserve-and-accelerate pattern replicates on two further non-abelian tasks
    ($1.76\times$ on $A_5$, $2.50\times$ on $D_{60}$ under defloored measurement)
    and at $100\%$ survival on modular-addition scales up to
    $p{=}337$ (a $1.18\times$ acceleration there), closing the single-task objection.
    Norm growth is therefore not necessary for grokking under Muon---and its
    removal accelerates it---a byproduct of orthogonalized updates rather than
    the generalization mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:causal}).
  \item \textbf{Growth-route signatures.} Memorization-to-grokking norm growth
    ($6.5\pm0.7\times$ on $S_5$, measured on the route-diagnostic arm; the
    uncapped runs of the norm-cap arm give $6.26\pm0.84\times$ independently),
    representation rotation ($70$--$80^\circ$), and later linear-mode-connectivity
    basin commitment (0/5 seeds locked by step 8000 vs.\ 2/5 for AdamW), measured
    as a descriptive cross-phenomenon signature.
  \item \textbf{A growth-floor argument} (Remark~\ref{prop:growthfloor})
    shows that the fixed per-step Frobenius energy of orthogonalized updates forces
    monotone norm growth under Muon and is absent under AdamW, giving the growth
    a sign-and-scaling rationale without predicting the empirical magnitude. We
    state it as a remark rather than a proposition because our own refit rejects
    its quantitative exponent.
  \item \textbf{Bounding phenomena.} Massive activations track effective step
    size, not per-update orthogonalization; plasticity under continual training
    extends---but does not cure---on a synthetic arm via a feature-rank (not
    weight-spectrum) dissociation that does not transfer to the permuted-MNIST
    benchmark (both in Section~\ref{sec:lmc}); a de-confounded group ladder plus
    the companion-reported SGDM trainability floor
    (Section~\ref{sec:floor}), which together license the ``orthogonalization not
    necessary'' reading, with a plain-Adam arm sharpening the family ordering into
    a spectrum (decay-free Adam fails selectively on the non-abelian rungs); and a
    five-arm update-geometry control (Section~\ref{sec:geom}) that localizes the
    effect to spectrum flattening on $S_5$ while showing the geometry sets only
    speed on modular addition. These bound the account rather than exemplify it.
\end{enumerate}
We do not adjudicate the spectral-geometry theory debate. The route is a
descriptive signature, not a claimed unique cause of generalization.
The published grokking-acceleration literature is thin
(Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}; Table~\ref{tab:accel-landscape}): a single controlled optimizer-swap result, a handful of
gradient-filtering methods, and an architectural transfer route via Kolmogorov--Arnold
representations \citep{park2025kagrokking}, none of which test what the acceleration causally depends
on. That gap is what this paper's norm-cap intervention fills. Figure~\ref{fig:scheme} summarizes the design and the causal chain.
Unlike the descriptive timing sweeps elsewhere in this line of work, this paper is
defined by a single primary causal manipulation---a downward-only Frobenius ceiling
that falsifies a specific named mechanism (norm growth), together with its two-sided
constant-norm-sphere control---and contains no timing-sweep
result; the precision$\times$objective$\times$decay characterization of the grokking
clock is a separate, observational study routed elsewhere.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:setup}

All phenomena share a 420k-parameter two-layer GrokTransformer, so that
optimizer-to-optimizer differences are not confounded by capacity. The phenomena
are: (i)~modular-addition grokking; (ii)~$S_5$ group composition (the non-abelian symmetric group on 5 elements);
(iii)~continual-learning plasticity; (iv)~attention-sink/massive activations; and
(v)~spawn-fork linear-mode connectivity. We compare three optimizers throughout:
AdamW (a contraction baseline via decoupled weight decay), Muon (orthogonalized
momentum), and SGDM (plain momentum); Muon and SGDM differ only in whether the
hidden-matrix momentum is orthogonalized, applied through a hybrid parameter split
so that embeddings and biases are handled identically across the two. The shared
hyperparameter configuration is given in Table~\ref{tab:hparams}.

The modular-addition and group-composition arms use full-batch updates on the
train split of size $0.4\,|\mathcal{X}|$; architecture defaults are
$d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}128$, $n_{\mathrm{heads}}{=}4$, $n_{\mathrm{layers}}{=}2$
(${\approx}420$k parameters). Per-phenomenon
probes are described in each section. Unless stated otherwise, reported quantities
are medians over seeds; the grokking delay is $T = T_{\mathrm{grok}} -
T_{\mathrm{mem}}$, where $T_{\mathrm{mem}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{grok}}$ are the first
steps at which train and validation accuracy cross threshold. Because evaluation
is periodic, every step-valued quantity is quantized to its arm's evaluation
grid: a reported grok step $T$ is the first evaluation past the crossing, so the
true crossing lies in $(T-\Delta,\,T]$ for cadence $\Delta$, and every step
ratio inherits this $\pm\Delta$ resolution; where a denominator sits at the
evaluation floor (e.g.\ modular addition under Muon), the ratio is reported only
as a bound. The
norm-controlled Muon arm (Section~\ref{sec:causal}) imposes a downward-only
per-matrix Frobenius ceiling $\|W\|\leq k\cdot\|W\|_{\mathrm{init}}$ that caps
growth without inflating weights, so it does not cancel decoupled weight decay
and isolates the effect of suppressing upward drift alone.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Two empirically separable routes to grokking}
\label{sec:routes}

On modular-addition grokking, AdamW at zero weight decay still groks (5/5; delay
ratio $50\pm38$). The spread is one-seed-driven: seed s2 sits at delay 93 versus
22--37 for the rest, so dropping s2 gives $29\pm8$. This is generalization without
contraction-via-decay. Muon, by contrast, follows a growth route with a far shorter
delay: the apparent ``near-zero'' Muon delay is an artifact of the 50-step
evaluation floor, and at an evaluation cadence of 5 steps the true Muon delay is
10--40 steps (ratio 1.1--2.0). This double-route classification is the empirical
foundation for what follows; the route is descriptive, not a claimed
mechanism (Fig.~\ref{fig:two_route}).

\subsection{Growth signature on non-abelian \texorpdfstring{$S_5$}{S5} and a causal test}
\label{sec:causal}

Every Muon grok event on $S_5$ shows memorization-to-grokking hidden-norm growth
of $6.5\pm0.7$ ($\|W_{\mathrm{hidden}}\|$ at $T_{\mathrm{grok}}$ over its value
at $T_{\mathrm{mem}}$; a different quantity from the end-of-run/initialization
ratio of the group-ladder discriminator, Fig.~\ref{fig:norm_disc}), versus
0/20 growth-signature events for AdamW and SGDM. Norm growth is thus the route
discriminator, not a necessity claim: AdamW does not grok in this specific $S_5$
route-diagnostic budget, although separate $S_5$ ladder controls show AdamW can grok
under other settings (a hyperparameter rescue map, Fig.~\ref{fig:rescue}, recovers
AdamW grokking on $S_5$ at high weight decay while SGDM never recovers). Three
AdamW--$S_5$ tallies recur in this paper and differ only in arm, not in
phenomenon: $0/5$ in this 20{,}000-step route-diagnostic arm, $0/8$ in the
equally short-budget sphere, fine-cadence, and rate-crossing controls
(Sections~\ref{sec:causal} and~\ref{sec:lrcross}), and $5/5$ on the
30{,}000-step dataset-size-controlled rung-120 ladder of
Section~\ref{sec:floor}, whose longer budget and dataset-size-controlled
training set are what recover AdamW. SGDM fails to
train $S_5$ at all---an optimizer-family floor, not a complexity threshold
(Section~\ref{sec:floor}; cf.\ \citealp{dragutinovic2026usemuon}). Representation
rotation of 70--80$^\circ$ accompanies it, but the rotation rate overlaps AdamW's, so
rotation is a noisy corroborator, not a clean discriminator. Grokking occurs at
effective rank 111/128, refuting a universal ``grokking $\Leftrightarrow$
rank-compression'' reading: the memorizing solution's stable rank is lower
(${\approx}23$).

\begin{mdframed}[style=propstyle,nobreak=true]
\begin{proposition}[Growth-floor for orthogonalized-update norm growth]
\label{prop:growthfloor}
\emph{Assumptions.}
(A1) Each hidden-matrix update is orthogonalized so that its applied increment is
$\Delta W = -\eta\,s\,U$ with $U$ semi-orthogonal ($\sigma_i(U)\approx 1$) and
$s = \max(1,\,d_{\mathrm{out}}/d_{\mathrm{in}})^{1/2}$ a fixed shape scale
(Newton--Schulz output; \citealp{jordan2024muon,bernstein2024modular}).
(A2) Weight decay is decoupled and small at the swept $\lambda$, removing
$\eta\lambda\|W\|$ per step.
(A3) Momentum $\beta=0.95$ induces positive step-to-step correlation
(coherence factor $\rho\in(0,1]$).
(A4) Curvature does not systematically anti-align increments with $W$
(no engineered contraction).

\emph{Claim.} Under (A1)--(A4):
(i)~the per-step Frobenius energy is fixed at
\begin{equation}
  \|\Delta W\|_F \;\approx\; \eta\, s\,\sqrt{r}, \qquad
  r = \mathrm{rank}(W) \approx \min(d_{\mathrm{out}},\,d_{\mathrm{in}}),
  \label{eq:frobenius-floor}
\end{equation}
independent of $\|W\|$; whereas AdamW's per-coordinate normalized step carries no
fixed spectral energy and, with decoupled decay, drives $\|W\|$ downward.
(ii)~In the worst case (decorrelated steps) the norm grows diffusively,
$\|W_t\|_F \gtrsim \sqrt{\|W_0\|_F^2 + \eta^2 s^2 r\, t}$; under (A3) the growth is
closer to ballistic (linear in $t$). Either way $\|W_t\|_F$ is monotone
non-decreasing until decay balances the injection at a fixed point
\begin{equation}
  \|W^*\|_F \;\propto\; \frac{s\,\sqrt{r}}{\lambda}
                         \;\times\; (\text{coherence factor}),
  \label{eq:balance-norm}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\|W^*\|_F \propto 1/\lambda$ at fixed momentum.
(iii)~The same argument gives no growth floor for AdamW: the balance point of a
contracting, non-fixed-energy step lies at or below $\|W_0\|$.

\emph{Scope / what is not claimed.} The magnitude of the empirical ratio
(the $6.5\pm0.7$ memorization-to-grokking growth on $S_5$) is not predicted: it depends on the step count to grokking
and on the coherence factor, both empirical. The statement is
sign-and-scaling, not a tight quantitative prediction.
Unit-singular-value increments also bias the weight spectrum toward uniformity,
predicting high effective rank under Muon---consistent with the measured effective
rank $111/128$ at grokking on $S_5$.
\end{proposition}
\end{mdframed}

\label{fig:two_route}
\end{figure}

Remark~\ref{prop:growthfloor} gives the growth a first-principles sign and a
$1/\lambda$ scaling; the norm term it produces is the optimizer-side counterpart of
the norm-based generalization bound of Oneto et al.\ \citep{neucom2026grokking}
(Section~\ref{sec:related}).

Remark~\ref{prop:growthfloor} predicts the Muon equilibrium hidden norm decreases
with weight decay ($\|W^*\|\propto1/\lambda$ at fixed momentum) while AdamW shows no
such scaling. Sweeping $\lambda$ from $0.001$ to $0.3$ confirms both in sign and
monotonicity (Fig.~\ref{fig:gap_normlaw}): the Muon norm follows a negative power law
(log--log slope $-0.43\pm0.03$, $R^2=0.87$; median $536\to57$) while AdamW is flat
(slope $0.03$, $R^2=0.31$). The Muon exponent is shallower than the idealized $-1$:
refitting the raw runs gives $-0.43\pm0.03$ at the final step ($-0.47\pm0.03$ over
the last quarter of training; $-0.52\pm0.03$ excluding the $\lambda{=}0.3$ floor),
which rejects the $-1$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:balance-norm} at ${>}12\sigma$ but is
statistically consistent with the $-1/2$ exponent that rotational-equilibrium
analysis predicts for the steady-state norm, $\|W^*\|\propto(\eta/\lambda)^{1/2}$
\citep{kosson2023rotational}. We therefore read Remark~\ref{prop:growthfloor}
as a heuristic sign-and-floor argument---it gets the direction, the monotonicity,
and the AdamW contrast right---while the rotational-equilibrium prediction is the
better quantitative account of the exponent.

The causal test settles the necessity question. Capping Muon's hidden-norm growth
with a downward-only ceiling $k\cdot\|W\|_{\mathrm{init}}$ preserves
grokking 8/8 at every $k$ including $k=1$ and accelerates it ${\sim}10{\times}$
(grok step $3525\to325$ on $S_5$; seed-level bootstrap 95\% CI $6.6$--$16.7\times$,
per-ratio CIs in Table~\ref{tab:ci}), and this replicates on modular addition (grok 8/8
at every ceiling with $\|W_{\mathrm{hidden}}\|$ capped ${\sim}12{\times}$; the
acceleration there is far smaller, $1.18\times$ (95\% percentile CI
$1.08$--$1.23$, BCa $1.09$--$1.27$; median $65\to55$), measured on the defloored
cadence-5 arm because Muon groks mod-add by step 100 even uncapped and the 50-step
grid cannot resolve the gap). Within the swept range of ceilings and
both tasks, norm growth is therefore not necessary for grokking under
Muon---consistent with a norm-clock reading (smaller weights grok faster) and with
the growth being a byproduct of the orthogonalized update rather than the
generalization mechanism: were the growth necessary, suppressing it would have
to delay or block grokking, and it does neither. The route is descriptive, now
backed by the manipulated-cause arm (Fig.~\ref{fig:normctl}; Table~\ref{tab:normctl}).
The ${\sim}10{\times}$ acceleration figure is a within-Muon comparison (norm-capped
vs.\ uncapped Muon runs); it does not compare Muon to AdamW.

The ${\sim}10{\times}$ $S_5$ acceleration does not transfer as a fixed magnitude. On
a second non-abelian task ($A_5$, rung-60) the cap preserves grokking (8/8 seeds) and
accelerates it $1.76\times$ (95\% CI $1.36$--$1.94\times$), and on a third ($D_{60}$,
rung-120) it preserves grokking
(5/5 seeds) and accelerates it $1.33\times$ at the 50-step evaluation cadence
(95\% CI $1.00$--$1.67\times$; Fig.~\ref{fig:gap_normcap}). That $D_{60}$ interval
touched $1.0$ only because the coarse grid could not resolve the difference: a
defloored arm at evaluation cadence 5 (8 seeds per condition) puts the ratio at
$2.50\times$ (median $200\to80$, 95\% percentile CI $2.24$--$2.97$, BCa
$2.26$--$3.00$), excluding $1.0$. Across all three non-abelian tasks the magnitude
is task-dependent and the preserve-and-accelerate direction invariant. The conclusion is also not a two-layer
artifact---re-running the $S_5$ dose-response at depth 4 preserves grokking at every
ceiling and reproduces the acceleration.

\paragraph{Two-sided control: constant-norm spheres for all three optimizers.} The cap
is one-sided (it only prevents growth), leaving open whether the norm level
rather than the update geometry carries the effect. A two-sided control closes this:
after every optimizer step, every hidden matrix is rescaled---up or down---to exactly
its initialization norm, for AdamW, Muon, and SGDM alike (the Omnigrok sphere
constraint \citep{liu2023omnigrok}, here applied uniformly at the standard
initialization scale; 8 seeds per cell, both tasks, pinned norms verified at
$12.5$ throughout). Three outcomes. First, Muon's speed survives the sphere intact:
sphere-constrained Muon groks $S_5$ in $8/8$ seeds at median step $375$ (range
$250$--$500$), against $3525$ for unconstrained Muon---a $9.4\times$ ratio
(95\% CI $6.6$--$16.4\times$), the same ${\sim}10\times$
acceleration as the $k=1$ cap---and still groks modular addition at step 100 (the
second 50-step evaluation), the same step as unconstrained Muon. With the norm trajectory eliminated entirely (no growth and no
contraction), the update geometry alone reproduces every Muon result. Second, the
sphere does not rescue the other optimizers on $S_5$: sphere-AdamW fails $0/8$ and
sphere-SGDM $0/8$, exactly as their unconstrained counterparts fail $0/5$, so their
$S_5$ failure is not a norm-level problem that constraint could fix. Third, on modular
addition---where AdamW does grok---the sphere accelerates AdamW ${\sim}3.4\times$
(median $3200\to950$; 95\% CI $1.7$--$7.6\times$) and tightens SGDM's seed spread ($250\,[150,5300]\to150\,[150,350]$):
for decay-route optimizers, part of the delay is time spent contracting the norm, and
fixing the norm removes that wait, reproducing Omnigrok's constant-norm observation
inside this testbed. Together with the cap, the dissociation is now two-sided: neither
suppressing Muon's growth nor abolishing norm dynamics altogether touches the
geometry-route advantage, while the same intervention shortcuts the decay-route clock.

\subsection{The SGDM floor as an optimizer-family property}
\label{sec:floor}

Throughout the growth-route results, SGDM serves as a negative control (it fails to
train $S_5$ and never locks a basin). Two lines of evidence establish that these
failures are an optimizer-family floor, not evidence that the harder tasks
require orthogonalization: a group ladder we run here (Fig.~\ref{fig:floor}), and
a trainability-cliff grid reported in full in a companion manuscript and
summarized below. First, a dataset-size-controlled
group ladder: holding group order $|G|^2$ fixed within each rung and varying only
commutator density, AdamW and Muon grok the entire abelian$\to$non-abelian ladder
(rung-60 $\{Z_{60}, D_{30}, A_5\}$, rung-120 $\{Z_{120}, D_{60}, S_5\}$)---Muon at
5/5 seeds on every rung, AdamW at 5/5 except $A_5$ (3/5)---while SGDM fails 0/5 on
every rung, including the abelian $Z_{60}$. Orthogonalization is therefore not necessary for the non-abelian
groups (both AdamW and Muon grok the whole abelian$\to$$S_5$ ladder); the only
necessity is SGDM's wholesale floor, which
already appears on the easiest abelian group, refuting a complexity-graded
orthogonalization-necessity threshold once dataset size is de-confounded. Second,
that floor has a sharp phase structure: on a Boolean degree task SGDM shows a
$(\text{width}\times\text{lr})$ trainability cliff, with a critical width of $256$
at learning rates $\leq0.03$ on the pure-degree-3 target, and the nested
degree-staircase target shifting that edge ${\sim}8\times$ toward lower width. The
cliff is SGDM-specific; the AdamW/Muon control shows none. We report this arm in
full, with the complete width $\times$ lr grid and its criterion-robustness
checks, in a companion manuscript; here it serves only as a bound on the
optimizer-family floor---which is why we read SGDM's failures throughout as a
floor, not a necessity threshold.

\paragraph{Plain Adam sits between the floor and AdamW: decay buys the non-abelian rungs.}
A decay-free arm sharpens the family ordering into a spectrum. Running plain Adam
(AdamW at $\lambda{=}0$; $d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}256$, $30$k steps, 5 seeds per group)
on the same ladder, the abelian and dihedral rungs still grok---slowly (median grok
step $1000$ on $Z_{120}$, $4300$ on $Z_{60}$, $12000$ on $D_{60}$, $22700$ on
$D_{30}$, the last near the step budget)---but both non-abelian rungs fail
completely: on $A_5$ and $S_5$ every seed memorizes without generalizing (train
accuracy $1.00$, test $\leq0.09$, $0/5$ grok). The resulting ordering---SGDM fails
the whole ladder; plain Adam groks only the abelian/dihedral rungs; AdamW with decay
groks everything, slowly; Muon groks everything near-instantly, capped or not---says
that weight decay is what buys the Adam family the non-abelian groups, whereas the
orthogonalized route needs neither decay nor norm growth there. Within this spectrum
the SGDM floor remains a family property, not a complexity threshold; the plain-Adam
failure, by contrast, is structure-selective.

\paragraph{Learning-rate crossing: no rate transplant closes the gap.}
\label{sec:lrcross}
The AdamW--Muon comparisons above are made at each optimizer's native
operating point (AdamW hidden-matrix lr $10^{-3}$, Muon $2\times10^{-2}$),
which leaves a rate-confound reading open: perhaps Muon's advantage is simply
a more favorable learning rate that AdamW was denied. A 64-run crossing
control tests this directly by running each optimizer at both hidden-matrix
rates on both tasks (8 fresh seeds per cell, standard $d{=}128$/depth-2
configuration and 20{,}000-step budget; Table~\ref{tab:lrcross}). On modular
addition every cell groks $8/8$ and Muon leads at every pairing: at native
rates the medians are $100$ vs $3375$ ($33.8\times$, 95\% CI $15.0$--$46.5$);
holding the hidden rate fixed across optimizers, Muon leads $8.4\times$
($3.9$--$11.6$) at $10^{-3}$ and $62.5\times$ ($28.5$--$107.5$) at
$2\times10^{-2}$; and with both rates transplanted the lead is $15.6\times$
($7.1$--$26.7$). Transplanting hurts both optimizers (Muon $100\to400$, AdamW
$3375\to6250$)---no rate assignment in the swept pair closes the gap. On
$S_5$ the crossing changes nothing for AdamW---$0/8$ at both rates within the
budget, consistent with unconstrained AdamW's $0/5$ at this short-budget
operating point (the longer-budget, dataset-size-controlled ladder above is
where AdamW does grok $S_5$)---while Muon groks $8/8$ (median $2625$) only at
its native rate and fails $0/8$ at $10^{-3}$. The honest reading cuts both
ways: the acceleration is a property of the (optimizer, learning-rate-regime)
pair---no learning-rate transplant rescues AdamW, so the advantage is not a
tuning artifact, but Muon's advantage is likewise not ``orthogonalization at
any rate,'' since on $S_5$ it requires its native regime.

\subsection{Boundary phenomena}

The remaining phenomena bound the growth-route account rather than exemplifying it;
each is measured against the same AdamW and SGDM controls.

\paragraph{Later basin commitment.}\label{sec:lmc}
In a spawn-fork linear-mode-connectivity experiment, at spawn step 8000 Muon's
inter-child loss barrier is uniformly high (all 5 seeds $>2.1$, median 3.18; 0/5
basins locked) while AdamW's cluster low (median 0.56; 2/5 locked)---a
distributional difference in basin commitment, not a clean ``$4{\times}$-later
/ $k^\star$'' claim (where $k^\star$ denotes the basin-merge threshold spawn step,
i.e.\ the first step at which the inter-child loss barrier drops below 0.1; this
did not survive five seeds; both children grok). Consistent with the
growth-route picture, weights keep growing and the basin appears to commit later in this
small train-pool audit. SGDM never learns
the task, so its low barriers are vacuous and excluded (its nominal 1/5 lock is
vacuous for the same reason). We report this as
underpowered ($n=5$; Fisher exact on lock-by-8000 gives $p\approx0.22$, directional)
and measured on the train pool---a held-out-basin version is a natural reviewer
request and is not yet run (Fig.~\ref{fig:lmc}).

\paragraph{Massive activations track effective step size.}\label{sec:sink}
At the sink arm's own operating point (AdamW at lr $10^{-3}$, Muon at
hidden-matrix lr $2\times10^{-2}$---the headline rates of
Table~\ref{tab:hparams}; the $3\times10^{-3}$/$5\times10^{-3}$ points below
belong to this section's learning-rate control sweep), Muon's massive-activation spike is large
(4787 vs.\ AdamW 245 vs.\ SGDM 15), but the learning-rate arm refutes an
optimizer-family ``amplification'': the spike magnitude is monotone in effective
step size within each family and the families overlap (AdamW @ lr $3\times
10^{-3}$ $\approx1440$; Muon @ lr $5\times10^{-3}$ $\approx195$, below AdamW's
stable spike). The signature is not ``orthogonalization amplifies'' but rather that
Muon trains stably at step sizes whose spikes sit 1--2 orders above AdamW's
stable range---an operating-point statement, not a per-update amplification. A
sandwich-norm architecture lever collapses the spike regardless of optimizer. This
bounds the ``orthogonalization amplifies activations'' account rather than
instantiating the route (Fig.~\ref{fig:sink}). At $n=3$ the peak activation also rises with depth within
each optimizer family (Fig.~\ref{fig:spike_depth}, an existence/spread check, not a
depth-scaling rate): from one to three layers the family ordering is preserved (Muon $\gg$ AdamW $\gg$ SGDM).

\paragraph{Extended (not cured) plasticity.}\label{sec:plasticity}
Under continual training, Muon is a plasticity extender, not a cure: it
pushes the censor onset (the first task index that fails to fit within the step
budget) from 7--34 to 75--81 (out of 100) and shallows the
loss-of-plasticity slope 6--12$\times$, but plasticity is eventually lost. The
dissociation: the weight spectrum stays preserved (0.91--0.98) while feature rank
collapses to zero---separating the weight-spectrum-regularization mechanism from the
operative one. This is a mechanism dissociation (the plasticity benefit has
no growth signature and runs through feature rank), so it bounds the route's
scope rather than instancing it (Fig.~\ref{fig:plasticity}).

\paragraph{Warm-start vs cold-start bridge (P5).} A bounded bridge arm separates
continual-state loss from intrinsic late-task difficulty. On the same projection-shift task
family, cold-start controls (fresh model per task) fit all 30/30 tasks quickly for every
optimizer, while the warm-start continual arm censors AdamW/SGDM late tasks and leaves Muon
as the only optimizer fitting all 30/30 tasks. Muon extends plasticity in
the continual state, but it is still slower than its own cold-start control, so it is not a
plasticity cure (Table~\ref{tab:p5bridge}, Fig.~\ref{fig:p5bridge}; both in
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{Benchmark boundary (permuted-MNIST).} The extend-plasticity result above is on
a synthetic shifting-projection arm. On the recognized permuted-MNIST loss-of-plasticity
benchmark \citep{dohare2024plasticity} it does not transfer (Fig.~\ref{fig:plast_scale}):
across 150 tasks Muon loses plasticity faster than AdamW at every learning rate
(fit-speed slowdown $4.0$--$6.1\times$ for Muon at lr $0.005$--$0.02$ versus $2.2\times$ for
AdamW and SGDM). A fresh seed-extension check raises the low-lr Muon controls to five
seeds each and preserves the boundary: the proposed feature-rank mechanism does not
hold---at well-scaled learning rates Muon keeps feature effective rank high ($224$--$232$
vs AdamW's $59$) with almost no dead units, yet still loses plasticity fastest
(the full per-task feature-rank trajectory is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:featrank}). (The catastrophic feature
collapse at lr $0.02$---rank $7$, $95\%$ dead units---is a separate over-step artifact,
ruled out by the learning-rate and seed-extension controls.) We therefore scope the synthetic extend-plasticity
finding to that arm; it does not generalize to permuted-MNIST, where if anything Muon is
worse. This bounds the route's reach: the orthogonalized update's continual-learning
behaviour is benchmark-dependent, not a universal plasticity benefit
(Fig.~\ref{fig:plast_scale}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{Which part of the update geometry matters, and where.}\label{sec:geom}
The cap and sphere arms remove the norm trajectory but leave Muon's orthogonalized
update intact, so they do not identify which feature of that update carries the
effect. A five-arm control substitutes the update geometry directly while holding
the parameter set and every other configuration field fixed (50 runs, 5 seeds per
cell, both tasks): exact-SVD spectrum flattening (a positive control that should
behave like Newton--Schulz), energy-matched random semi-orthogonal updates
(same per-step Frobenius energy, no gradient information), spectrum-inverted
updates (gradient directions retained, singular values inverted), plus Muon and
AdamW references. On $S_5$ the arms separate sharply. Spectrum flattening
reproduces Muon (5/5 seeds grok, median step $3800$ vs $3950$, a ratio of
$0.96\times$), while the energy-matched random arm never groks ($0/5$, final test
accuracy $0.165$), the spectrum-inverted arm never groks ($0/5$, accuracy
$0.055$), and AdamW never groks ($0/5$). On modular addition every arm groks
$5/5$ and only the speed changes: Muon and spectrum flattening at step $100$,
spectrum-inverted at $2950$, AdamW at $3450$, and the random arm slowest at
$5700$. The reading is task-dependent: on $S_5$ the spectrum-flattening geometry
is necessary---neither energy injection nor gradient alignment with an inverted
spectrum groks at all---while on modular addition every structured update reaches
the solution and the geometry only sets the speed. This is the scope behind the
claim that the optimizer-dependence reduces to update geometry: it is a statement
about the task where the optimizer choice decides whether grokking happens, not a
claim that geometry governs speed everywhere. Because all five arms run through
the Muon code path, the per-run \texttt{\_meta} logs record
\texttt{optimizer:muon} throughout; the arm assignment for each run is recorded in
the batch \texttt{MANIFEST} as a per-run name-to-arm mapping.

\subsection{Synthesis: growth-route signatures and two bounding dissociations}
\label{sec:synthesis}

The descriptive non-contraction signature has three components, all under the
double-route frame of Section~\ref{sec:routes}: hidden-norm growth and
representation rotation (Section~\ref{sec:causal}), together with later basin
commitment (Section~\ref{sec:lmc}). The remaining two phenomena in the bounding
subsection are deliberately not route signatures: massive activations track the
operating point / effective step size, and plasticity is a feature-rank dissociation
on the synthetic arm, which does not transfer to permuted-MNIST. These instead bound
competing accounts (refuting ``orthogonalization
amplifies activations'' and, on the synthetic arm, ``weight-spectrum regularization
explains plasticity''---while the permuted-MNIST boundary shows even that benefit is
not universal). We do not claim the route is the unique cause of
generalization, and the manipulated-cause arm (Section~\ref{sec:causal}) shows the
growth is not necessary (and that its removal accelerates). The signature is therefore reported as a reproducible,
descriptive cross-phenomenon signature that locates the optimizer-dependence of the
grokking delay in the update geometry rather than in weight size, with the
geometry control of Section~\ref{sec:geom} narrowing that to the spectrum-flattening
component on $S_5$ (the signatures and dissociations are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:route-signatures} and Fig.~\ref{fig:synth};
Table~\ref{tab:evidence-ledger} collects each claim with its evidence and scope boundary).

\paragraph{Grokking: mechanisms and weight norms.}
Grokking was introduced by \citet{power2022grokking} on modular arithmetic tasks.
\citet{nanda2023progress} reverse-engineered the generalizing algorithm in modular-addition
transformers via mechanistic interpretability, identifying discrete Fourier-space circuits---whose eventual dominance under weight decay is explained by circuit-efficiency competition \citep{varma2023circuiteff}---
and continuous ``excluded loss'' progress measures that underlie the sharp phase
transition. Omnigrok \citep{liu2023omnigrok} demonstrated that constraining weight
norms (via an init-scale sphere) accelerates generalization across a range of tasks,
placing norm control at the center of grokking dynamics. Later work connects
the delay to a norm-separation law \citep{khanh2026normseparation} and
studies interventions that change the delay via entropy collapse
\citep{khanh2026entropycollapse}---the latter delays grokking, in contrast to our
downward-only cap that accelerates it.  Norm-matched controls and weight-decay regime
diagnostics \citep{verma2026weightdecay} further pin the delay to the rate at which
weight decay contracts the norm. Oneto et al.\ \citep{neucom2026grokking} reconcile
grokking with statistical learning theory analytically, bounding generalization
through norm- and stability-based quantities and identifying the weight norm as the
controlling axis. Our result supplies the complementary causal-interventional leg on
that same axis: where their bound is descriptive of the norm's role, our
downward-only cap manipulates it and shows the growth is not the operative signal.
The balance-norm scaling of Remark~\ref{prop:growthfloor}
(Eq.~\ref{eq:balance-norm}) is the optimizer-side counterpart of the norm term in
their bound.

\paragraph{Muon and orthogonalized/spectral optimizers.}
Muon orthogonalizes each update via Newton--Schulz iteration; its theoretical
foundation is modular duality \citep{bernstein2024modular}, which constructs
operator-norm-based duality maps for each layer type.
\citet{tveit2025muongrokking} documented Muon's grokking acceleration on a bare
hyperparameter grid; our work adds the cross-phenomenon mechanism and the causal
norm-cap intervention.  The flat/uniform-spectrum dynamics account
\citep{beneventano2026spectraldynamics} and the skeptical ``alignment suffices''
result \citep{shumaylov2026muonnotspecial} offer competing theoretical interpretations
we do not adjudicate; we provide empirical signatures instead.
Spectral flattening \citep{nguyen2026spectralflattening} and simplicity bias
\citep{dragutinovic2026usemuon} round out the mechanistic picture of why Muon's
equalized learning rates stabilize training.

\paragraph{Positioning the growth route against published Muon--grokking accounts.}
Four prior lines directly intersect our findings; we state where we agree,
extend, or diverge.

\emph{vs.\ \citet{tveit2025muongrokking}.}
Tveit et al.\ document Muon's grokking acceleration (${\approx}1.5\times$ across seven
modular-arithmetic tasks) but report it at the optimizer level without an interventional
test of the cause. Our causal norm-cap supplies that test: suppressing growth preserves
grokking 8/8 and accelerates it ${\sim}10{\times}$ on $S_5$, establishing that the growth
is not necessary---a byproduct. Their controlled-swap evidence rests on this single study (larger accelerations
elsewhere are gradient-filtering, a distinct category; no onset measurement exists for
second-order optimizers such as Shampoo/SOAP), so our lever is a separate one rather than a
point in a meta-distribution.

\emph{vs.\ \citet{beneventano2026spectraldynamics} and
\citet{shumaylov2026muonnotspecial}.}
Beneventano et al.\ attribute Muon's behaviour to flat-spectrum dynamics; Shumaylov et al.\
argue alignment and step-size optimality suffice. We do not adjudicate. Our
massive-activation result (Section~\ref{sec:sink}), where spike magnitude tracks effective
step size rather than orthogonalization, aligns with the latter's deflationary stance, but
the growth route and causal cap are a separable signature neither account predicts.

\emph{vs.\ \citet{kumar2024lazytorich}.}
Kumar et al.\ frame grokking as a lazy-to-rich transition driven by regularization. We are
compatible: orthogonalized updates inject near-uniform-singular-value steps that push the
spectrum toward high effective rank (Remark~\ref{prop:growthfloor}; $111/128$ at
grokking on $S_5$). Our addition is the norm-route---growth driven by each step's fixed
Frobenius energy, with the causal cap showing the growth itself is non-causal---a
mechanistic layer on their picture, not a competitor.

\emph{vs.\ \citet{liu2023omnigrok}.}
Omnigrok constrains weight norms to a symmetric sphere and speeds grokking; our
intervention is a downward-only ceiling on a Muon run already on the growth route. The
direction agrees (smaller norms grok faster) but the mechanism differs: their sphere is a
constant constraint from initialization, whereas our cap suppresses only upward drift.
Norm-matched controls that delay grokking \citep{khanh2026entropycollapse} show norm level
alone is insufficient, so the operative signal is something the cap leaves intact rather
than norm size.

\paragraph{Implicit bias, weight decay, and norm growth.}
Decoupled weight decay \citep{loshchilov2019adamw} --- whose late-phase implicit bias provably induces grokking transitions \citep{lyu2024dichotomy} --- separates the $\ell_2$-penalty
contraction from adaptive gradient scaling, making AdamW's norm-contracting behaviour
qualitatively distinct from Muon's fixed-energy-injection regime.  The rotational
equilibrium framework \citep{kosson2023rotational} characterizes how weight decay
balances the magnitude and angular update of each neuron, providing a per-neuron
implicit-bias lens complementary to our population-level norm-growth signature.
Ortega and Masegosa \citep{neucom2026sgdbias} frame optimizer implicit bias via
large-deviation theory---the analytic register our growth-floor argument complements
empirically. Edge-of-Stability dynamics
\citep{cohen2021eos,cohen2022adaptiveeos} show that adaptive optimizers also
operate near the sharpness threshold $2/\eta$, relevant background for the
operating-point interpretation of Muon's large effective step sizes
(Section~\ref{sec:sink}).

\paragraph{Linear mode connectivity and basin geometry.}
\citet{frankle2020lmc} established that networks optimized from the same initialization
under different SGD noise converge to linearly connected minima from early in training---a
property we use as a basin-commitment probe.  \citet{entezari2022permutation} conjecture
that accounting for permutation symmetries removes loss barriers between independently
trained networks, motivating our spawn-fork protocol that keeps the parent network
fixed to control for permutation misalignment. The train-pool LMC result
(Section~\ref{sec:lmc}) is reported as underpowered; held-out-basin and
permutation-aligned versions are natural next steps.

\paragraph{Massive activations and attention sinks.}
\citet{sun2024massive} characterize massive activations---a small number of dimensions
with values ${\sim}10^5$ times the median---as implicit bias terms in large LLMs
whose location is fixed regardless of input; subsequent work shows such sinks emerge as a function of the optimization configuration rather than model scale \citep{gu2025attnsink}. Our Section~\ref{sec:sink} reports a
qualitatively similar spike in our 420k-parameter toy transformer; the key
distinction is that on our testbed the spike tracks effective step size
(an operating-point property) rather than model scale, and a sandwich-norm
architecture lever eliminates it.

\paragraph{Loss of plasticity.}
\citet{dohare2024plasticity} established the permuted-MNIST benchmark for loss of
plasticity in continual learning. Our Section~\ref{sec:plasticity} reproduces and
extends the benchmark: on the standard task Muon loses plasticity faster
than AdamW, bounding the scope of the synthetic-arm extension result.  The
benchmark boundary is a deliberate scope statement, not a contradiction
of the synthetic result.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the growth route is and is not.}
The central empirical finding is that Muon generalizes along a non-contraction
route: weights grow monotonically, representations rotate, and the loss basin stays
open longer, in sharp contrast to AdamW's decay-driven contraction.
Remark~\ref{prop:growthfloor} gives this a heuristic floor: the fixed
per-step Frobenius energy of an orthogonalized update must accumulate monotonically
when weight decay is small, so norm growth is structurally forced by the optimizer's
geometry \citep{bernstein2024modular}.  The norm-cap intervention then answers the
necessity question directly (Section~\ref{sec:causal}).  This
distinguishes our result from Omnigrok \citep{liu2023omnigrok}, whose symmetric
constant-norm sphere prevents growth from the start and whose inference about
norm control is correlational rather than interventional.

\paragraph{What the cap does and does not license (causal preemption).}
A one-sided cap shows that fast generalization proceeds when the growth is
suppressed; by itself this does not prove the growth is inert on the natural,
uncapped trajectory---an intervention can in principle preempt one mechanism by
enabling another. Three observations close most of that gap. First, the
dose-response is monotone: intermediate ceilings interpolate smoothly between
the uncapped and $k=1$ endpoints (Table~\ref{tab:normctl}), and the defloored
fresh-seed replication extends the monotone ladder with a new $k{=}4$ point
($3.3\times\to6.6\times\to10.3\times$; Section~\ref{sec:causal})---which a
switch to a qualitatively different mechanism at some ceiling would not
predict. Second, the
two-sided constant-norm sphere control removes the norm trajectory
entirely---no growth, no contraction, norm pinned at initialization for every
optimizer---and Muon's advantage survives unchanged while AdamW and SGDM are
not rescued on $S_5$; whatever carries the optimizer difference, it is not the
norm dynamics that these interventions manipulate. Third, the acceleration
under the cap is exactly the direction the norm-clock reading of the uncapped
dynamics predicts, rather than an anomaly requiring a new mechanism. We
therefore state the conclusion as a necessity claim---the growth is not
necessary, and its removal accelerates---strengthened by the sphere's
elimination result, not as a claim that the growth plays no role of any kind on
the natural trajectory.

\paragraph{Connecting to weight decay and implicit bias.}
AdamW's contraction route is mechanically explained by decoupled weight decay
\citep{loshchilov2019adamw}: the $\lambda\|W\|$ shrinkage term dominates unless
the gradient is large enough to overcome it, driving the weight norm down.  Muon
has the same decay term, but its fixed-energy-injection dynamic
(Eq.~\ref{eq:frobenius-floor}) more than offsets it at the learning rates swept here,
producing the falling equilibrium norm of Eq.~\ref{eq:balance-norm} (idealized
$\propto 1/\lambda$; the measured exponent is ${\approx}-0.5$, quantitatively
closer to the rotational-equilibrium prediction $\|W^*\|\propto(\eta/\lambda)^{1/2}$,
Section~\ref{sec:causal}).
The rotational equilibrium framework \citep{kosson2023rotational}, and more broadly the reading of weight decay as a controller of effective optimization dynamics rather than a regularizer \citep{dangelo2024wd}, provides a
complementary per-neuron view: at steady state weight decay balances the angular
and radial update components; under Muon the fixed-magnitude semi-orthogonal increment
shifts that equilibrium point outward, placing our route signature
within a coherent implicit-bias picture---and, on our measurements, supplying
the better quantitative fit to the equilibrium-norm exponent.

\paragraph{Relation to grokking mechanisms.}
The mechanistic interpretability of grokking on modular arithmetic
\citep{nanda2023progress} shows that the generalizing algorithm uses discrete Fourier
circuits and that progress toward it is continuous, though hidden.  Our findings
neither confirm nor refute that circuit account---we observe that Muon reaches the
generalized solution faster and with larger norms, not that it discovers a different
circuit.  Two separate comparisons are in play here. Across optimizers, Muon reaches
the circuit faster than AdamW while carrying larger norms; within Muon, the norm-cap
result shows that smaller norms after grokking (12.5 vs.\ 309.6 for seed 0) go with
faster, not slower, grokking. A clock interpretation reconciles them: weight decay
needs less time to bring a capped norm down to the generalizing threshold, analogous
to Omnigrok's sphere constraint, so it is the update geometry rather than the final
norm size that sets the speed. The two-sided sphere control (Section~\ref{sec:causal})
closes this loop directly: with every optimizer pinned to the same constant norm at
every step, Muon keeps its full advantage while AdamW's mod-add clock shortens by the
predicted contraction-wait, and the $S_5$ failures of AdamW and SGDM persist---the
clock reading survives a complete removal of norm dynamics.

\paragraph{Scope of the plasticity and activation results.}
The plasticity finding illustrates the importance of benchmark scope
\citep{dohare2024plasticity}: Muon extends plasticity on a synthetic shifting-projection
arm but loses it faster on permuted-MNIST.  The feature-rank dissociation (weight
spectrum preserved, feature rank collapses) is an honest mechanism diagnostic---it
shows that the plasticity benefit does not run through weight-spectrum regularization
on the synthetic arm, but this mechanism may not be operative on permuted-MNIST at
all.  Similarly, the massive-activation result \citep{sun2024massive} is
scale-dependent: at 420k parameters the spike is an operating-point artifact rather
than a model-scale emergent property, and it disappears under a sandwich-norm
architecture.  Both bounding dissociations sharpen the scope of the growth-route
claim rather than contradicting it.

\paragraph{Real-text pretraining boundary.}
\label{sec:realtext}
To locate the boundary of the cap result outside the grokking regime, we ran the
same intervention on ordinary byte-level language-model pretraining: decoder-only
transformers of 10.0M parameters on TinyStories \citep{eldan2023tinystories}
($0.30$B tokens) and 57.2M parameters on a FineWeb-Edu sample
\citep{penedo2024fineweb} ($0.60$B tokens), arms \{uncapped Muon, AdamW,
capped Muon $k\in\{1,2\}$\} plus the constant-norm sphere at 10M, 3 seeds per arm
(hybrid split with Muon hidden-matrix rate $10^{-2}$ and AdamW rate
$3\times10^{-4}$ from a short probe, weight decay $0.01$; a config audit
confirms the arms within each family differ only in the norm constraint; the two
families are analyzed separately throughout). Precision here is bf16 autocast
over fp32 master parameters, not a pure-bf16 parameter path, so the optimizer
state and the decoupled decay multiplier are applied in fp32 and the nominal
$\lambda{=}0.01$ takes effect as written. The direction inverts
(Table~\ref{tab:realtext}): at matched token counts, capping Muon's norm growth
costs validation loss dose-dependently. At 10M the median final cross-entropy
rises from $0.582$ (uncapped) to $0.663$ at $k{=}2$ and $0.768$ at $k{=}1$; at
57M from $0.955$ to $1.045$ and $1.162$---a paired per-seed cost of
$+0.08$--$0.09$ nats at $k{=}2$ and $+0.19$--$0.21$ at $k{=}1$, in 3/3 seeds and
nearly identical across the two families. The cap binds essentially throughout
($k{=}1$ from initialization, $k{=}2$ within the first ${\sim}5\%$ of the token
budget), while uncapped Muon grows its hidden norm to $6.4\times$/$5.0\times$
initialization by the end of the run. The sphere control at 10M lands on the
$k{=}1$ cap, not on uncapped Muon (median $0.767$ vs.\ $0.768$; per-seed gaps
$<0.005$ nats): on real text there is no one-sided-vs-two-sided dissociation
---any constraint holding the hidden norm at initialization scale pays the same
price. (A preliminary pooled read had suggested the sphere matched uncapped
Muon; that was an artifact of pooling the two families' different loss scales
and is refuted by the per-family analysis.) Muon's early-phase advantage does
transfer: at matched tokens Muon leads AdamW from the first evaluation in both
families (e.g.\ $1.06$ vs.\ $1.91$ at 4M tokens on the 10M model), and the lead
persists to the end of the 10M budget ($0.582$ vs.\ $0.595$, 3/3 seeds) but
inverts at 57M, where AdamW ends slightly ahead ($0.932$ vs.\ $0.955$, 3/3
seeds; AdamW is also $18$--$32\%$ faster in wall-clock throughput at these
sizes, so the token-matched comparison favors Muon). The scoping is therefore
two-way: in the grokking regime, suppressing norm growth preserves and
accelerates generalization; in ordinary pretraining at 10--57M parameters the
same intervention monotonically costs loss. The dispensability-and-acceleration
claim is a grokking-regime result, not a general norm-control recommendation.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}

All results are toy-scale (420k parameters, two layers; modular addition / $S_5$ /
continual learning / attention sink / linear-mode connectivity). The route is a
descriptive signature, causal only where a dissociation or intervention arm supports
it (Section~\ref{sec:causal} rank $+$ the norm cap---the strongest claim, robust
across six ceiling levels ($k\in\{\infty,4,3,2,1.5,1\}$, the $k{=}4$ point from
the defloored fresh-seed replication), four tasks ($S_5$, modular addition, $A_5$, $D_{60}$),
network depths 2 and 4, a $4\times$ width range ($d_{\mathrm{model}}=128$--$512$ on
$S_5$), and three modular-addition scales ($p=97$--$337$); Section~\ref{sec:plasticity}
spectrum/feature). The real-text arm (Section~\ref{sec:realtext}) bounds the
transfer of the cap result: at 10--57M parameters on ordinary byte-level
pretraining the cap costs loss rather than accelerating, so none of the
preserve-and-accelerate claims extend beyond the grokking regime; that arm is
itself limited ($n{=}3$ seeds, one weight-decay setting, $\le$57M parameters,
$\le$0.6B tokens). The Section~\ref{sec:lmc} LMC result---the weakest claim in the
paper---is a distributional basin
difference at $n=5$ (underpowered, Fisher $p\approx0.22$), measured on the train
pool, not a clean $k^\star/4{\times}$ claim. Fresh isolated short audits completed two
extra Muon fork seeds at spawn steps 0/50/100 (seeds 10--11) and again found no
$k^\star$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:lmc-extension}); three additional seeds (12--14) confirm this
non-hit at the combined $n=5$ short-audit level
(Fig.~\ref{fig:lmc-n5}), but the remaining densification to $n=15$ is still disproportionate
(each full Muon fork cell $\approx75$ min), so the LMC statement remains boundary-grade;
held-out-basin and permutation-aligned LMC \citep{entezari2022permutation,ainsworth2023gitrebasin} are the
natural way to settle it further. The
Section~\ref{sec:sink} activation result is for spiking configurations only (5/16
Muon runs do not spike high) and is operating-point-dependent. The
Section~\ref{sec:floor} ``SGDM necessity'' is not complexity-specific---SGDM fails
wholesale even on abelian groups (an optimizer-family floor plus a data-size
confound \citep{dragutinovic2026usemuon}), so we frame it as ``plain momentum cannot
train these families,'' not ``$S_5$ requires orthogonalization.'' All
optimizer comparisons are scoped to the swept learning-rate pair: the crossing
control (Section~\ref{sec:lrcross}) rules out a rate-transplant rescue of
AdamW, but it also shows Muon's advantage is a property of the (optimizer,
learning-rate-regime) pair rather than of orthogonalization at any rate---on
$S_5$, Muon itself fails at the AdamW-native rate. There is no indexed-journal
Muon-optimizer precedent, so we enter via the grokking-dynamics / implicit-bias
framing.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
The question that matters most for generality is whether the causal cap behaves the
same way on larger language-model pretraining, where the orthogonalized update is
actually deployed \citep{liu2025muonscale}; if the growth-as-byproduct conclusion
survives at scale, it would have practical consequences for norm-control heuristics.
The width $\times$ depth and modulus grids of Section~\ref{sec:causal} are a first
step in that direction---the cap's effect is, if anything, larger at higher
capacity---and the real-text boundary arm (Section~\ref{sec:realtext}) now gives
a first answer at 10--57M parameters: adverse. There, the norm freedom the cap
removes is load-bearing, and the open question sharpens from whether the regimes
separate to where---at what point between memorization-then-generalization
dynamics and ordinary pretraining the growth becomes load-bearing, and whether
tuned decay or schedules move that point.
Separately, the SGDM floor (Section~\ref{sec:floor}) is an optimizer-family property
unrelated to complexity; the plain-Adam arm has now sharpened that taxonomy into a
spectrum (decay-free Adam fails selectively on the non-abelian rungs), and extending
it to further non-orthogonalizing optimizers would map the spectrum more finely.

\section{Bootstrap confidence intervals for the headline acceleration ratios}
\label{app:ci}

Every acceleration ratio reported in the paper is a ratio of group medians of
per-seed grok steps. Table~\ref{tab:ci} gives seed-level nonparametric bootstrap
95\% confidence intervals for each of them ($2\times10^{4}$ resamples,
independent resampling within each arm; percentile and bias-corrected
accelerated (BCa) intervals). Grok steps are quantized to each arm's evaluation
grid (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), so interval endpoints land on ratios of grid
medians; the intervals are computed from the raw per-run logs, not from derived
tables. All $S_5$ intervals exclude 1 by a wide margin. On the coarse 50-step
grid the $D_{60}$ lower endpoint touches 1.0 and the modular-addition interval is
degenerate at the evaluation floor; the two defloored cadence-5 rows below the
rule resolve both, giving $2.50\times$ [2.24, 2.97] on $D_{60}$ and $1.18\times$
[1.08, 1.23] on modular addition, each excluding 1.0. The rows below the rule are
the fresh-seed revision arms: the defloored fine-cadence dose ladder and the
defloored $D_{60}$/modular-addition pair (evaluation cadence 5;
Section~\ref{sec:causal}) and the learning-rate crossing control
(Section~\ref{sec:lrcross}), computed with the identical bootstrap; with $n=8$
and tie-heavy medians the BCa endpoints of the defloored $k{=}1$ row are
coarse, so the percentile interval is the one quoted inline.
Table~\ref{tab:ci-seeds} lists the per-seed
grok steps behind the two independent estimates of the nominal
$d{=}128$/depth-2 $S_5$ ratio ($10.8\times$ and $15.9\times$): the batches agree
within seed noise (Mann--Whitney $p=0.56$ uncapped / $p=0.88$ capped; $2.8\%$ of
the log-step variance is between batches; the bootstrap CI of the ratio
difference, $-7.6$ to $+12.7$, spans zero).
